# Now Accepting: design direction

## The idea
The product is the picture. The first screen is a working search console over real data: choose care type, insurance and city, and see one signal dot per real provider plus the first three to call.

## How it's built
- **Direction:** "The product performs" (tasteskill). The hero blueprint was replaced because its controls adjusted headline type, which doesn't apply here. The footer keeps full links for navigation and the crisis line.
- **Colour:** healthcare teal and blue on cool white, with a deep navy console. Signal cyan appears only on data and live status.
- **Type:** Archivo, self-hosted under the OFL.
- **Pictures:** none, by owner's choice. Real interface fragments and data carry the page.
- **Shapes:** small 4px radius, tone steps instead of shadows, and a soft glow only on lit signal dots.

## Rules that keep providers comfortable
- The score rates a listing's public data, never quality of care. Say so wherever it could be misread.
- No red next to a clinician's name. The low level reads "Call to confirm", in neutral grey.
- Insurer names are plain text. Logos could imply a partnership we don't have.
- Paying never changes a score, and sponsored placements are labelled.

## What the category does
Reviewed Oct 2026: Grow Therapy, Talkiatry, Alma, SonderMind, Rula, Headway, One Medical, Psychology Today (Zocdoc and Healthgrades blocked automated access).
Nearly all use a lifestyle photo of a person, a soft serif headline ("...covered by insurance"), search pills and a row of insurer logos. They sell calm and care. None makes accuracy visible.

## Evidence
- First impressions form in about 50ms and carry over to judgments of credibility (Lindgaard et al., 2006): https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html
- Show a real organization, match design to purpose, and make claims verifiable: https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines
- 62% don't trust their health plan for finding care; 66% would trust it more with accurate directories (vendor-commissioned): https://www.beckerspayer.com/payer/62-of-americans-dont-trust-their-health-plan-when-looking-for-care-survey-finds.html
- Practices spend about $1,000 a month maintaining directories (CAQH, 2019): https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/caqh-survey-maintaining-provider-directories-costs-us-physician-practices-2-76-billion-annually-300957624.html
- Clients judge therapists from directory photos and wording (doctoral thesis, 22 interviews): https://research.brighton.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/what-factors-influence-a-clients-choice-of-counsellor-or-psychoth/

Evidence on what clinicians want from directory profiles was thin. Interview a few providers before relying on the provider-facing assumptions here.
